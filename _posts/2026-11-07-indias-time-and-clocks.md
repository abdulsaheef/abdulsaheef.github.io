This report brings together the historical development of regional clocks, railway and telegraph standardisation, post-independence policy, and a possible future arrangement that balances national coordination with local daylight.\

> **IMPORTANT: Proposal status**  \

## Executive Summary\
\
India currently follows one official time zone: **Indian Standard Time (IST), UTC+05:30**, based on the standard meridian of **82\'b030\uc0\u8242  E**, near Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh.\
\
India's east\'96west geographical span creates a substantial difference between local solar time in its western and eastern regions. A single national clock simplifies administration, transport, communication and national coordination, but it does not align equally well with daylight across the country.\
\
India did not always operate under one uniform clock. During British rule, regional standards such as Bombay Time, Calcutta Time and Madras Time coexisted. Madras time became particularly important to railway and telegraph operations. The movement towards a common standard culminated in the adoption and implementation of IST around 1905\'961906. Calcutta retained a separate time until 1948, while Bombay's municipal authority adopted IST in 1950.\
\
The proposal developed in this discussion retains IST as the national reference while introducing three regional civil-time standards:\
\
| Proposed zone | Offset from IST | UTC offset | Reference meridian |\
| --- | --- | --- | --- |\
| Bombay Time (BT) | IST \uc0\u8722  00:45 | UTC+04:45 | 71\'b015\u8242  E |\
| Madras Time (MT) | IST \uc0\u8722  00:15 | UTC+05:15 | 78\'b045\u8242  E |\
| Calcutta Time (CT) | IST + 00:30 | UTC+06:00 | 90\'b000\uc0\u8242  E |\
\
The proposed system uses longitude to establish reference meridians and approximate zone boundaries. It keeps IST for national coordination and uses regional time for local daily life.\
\
> **NOTE: Central principle**  \
> India could retain one national time reference while allowing regional civil time to reflect local geography and daylight more closely.\
